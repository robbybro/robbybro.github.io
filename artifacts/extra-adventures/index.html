Extra Adventures

A guest guide to Seattle and the PNW · July 30 – August 1, 2027 · Bainbridge Island, WA

We know a lot of you are traveling from out of town and in case you have some extra time and want to make a trip out of it here are a few of our favorite things in and around Seattle to keep you caffeinated, fed, and busy. If you are in doubt, reach out and we can try and steer you in the right direction.

For general food/drink recs check out Robby’s food website - the “Try” list is places we haven’t been and want to go, and the “True” list is places we’ve tried and liked

Getting around Seattle

We have all the rideshares you could ever want (Uber, Lyft, Waymo) but note that we also have the highest rideshare prices in the country. But fear not because we’ve got a great Lightrail system that connects the airport to the downtown corridor (the 1 Line which runs North/South), and recently expanded East/West with the 2 Line. We’ve also got all the Lime / Bird bikes and scooter brands too. There are busses too but I wouldn’t dare lol.

Day Trips

A few options that can be part of a day trip (or overnight) on the way north.

Bellingham out and back

Start with a hike to Oyster Dome for a fabulous view, stop at Taylor Shellfish Farms for oysters, then venture into town for amazing coffee at Makeworth and even more good food. Top it off with a beer at Structures.

Route map ↗

Whidbey Island Loop

Drive up to Deception Pass (great short hikes with beautiful views), and stop in Edison along the way for great bread (and weirdly good peanut butter), and a lovely diner. Take the Deception Pass bridge and work your way down to Greenbank Farm (very tasty loganberry pies) and Coupeville (fun town to explore) before taking the ferry back to Mukilteo and driving back to Seattle.

Route map ↗

Float the Snoqualmie River

We do this with friends every year! Start here and end here. You can rent floaties and get a ride back to your car through this service. Heads up: the start-point lot needs a Discover Pass and fills by about 10:30 AM on weekends, so carpool and leave most cars at the end point. The Fall City Floating shuttle drops you 0.2 miles from the put-in. Plan on 3–5 hours on the water.

Mt Rainier National Park

Our tallest mountain and one of 5 volcanoes over 10,000 feet. Ok, ok TECHNICALLY Mt St Helens is a little shorter than that now but that’s sorta recent. The park has all levels of hikes from leisurely strolls to ice-axe-and-crampon rope lines. Choose your own adventure.

More than 130 wineries across four districts, about a 30-minute drive from downtown Seattle.

Boeing Factory Tour & The Museum of Flight

These two are in opposite directions from downtown Seattle. Boeing Future of Flight in Mukilteo is about 25 miles north. Its Boeing Everett Factory Tour is an 80-minute guided tour that looks at airplane production from the factory balcony. You must be at least 4 feet tall, and phones and cameras are not allowed. The Museum of Flight is south of downtown on East Marginal Way.

Multi day trips

Worth building a few extra days around. Temps are typical late-July highs and lows.

Orcas Island

recommend 2 nightslate July ~71°/49°F, mild and dry

Orcas Island is our favorite of the San Juans. It’s also the hilliest!

How to get there
  • The ferry is a 90 min drive from Seattle and fills up quick, we recommend either biking on or getting a ferry reservation.
What to do
  • Drive/bike/hike Mt Constitution (there is hiking here)
  • Eat at Houlme
  • Get pastries at Seabird (only open Saturdays. Get there early, they sell out fast!)
  • Paddle board on Cascade Lake and walk the path that goes around the lake

San Juan Island

recommend 2 nightslate July ~71°/49°F, mild and dry

San Juan Island has some heavy hitters.

How to get there
  • Same ferry terminal and reservation rigamarole as Orcas.
What to do
  • Friday Harbor is a bit touristy but some very cute shops
  • Whale watching - plenty of options that leave from this island and one of the best areas for Orcas. If this is all you do you can walk onto the ferry as the town (Friday Harbor) is right off of the ferry and make this a day trip
  • Roche Harbor has some cute shops and good food
  • Scenic views of the San Juans, potential whale sightings, and hiking at San Juan County Park (we’re 2 for 2 on whale sightings during bikepacking trips here), Cattle Point, Lime Kiln Point State Park

Olympic Peninsula

late July ~70°/53°F in Port Townsend, ~72°/50°F at the Hoh

Home to the only rainforest in the lower 48, one of our favorite national parks, lavender farms as far as the eye can see, and some of the best pad thai we’ve ever had, the Olympic Peninsula is a vibe.

How to get there
  • Kingston or Bremerton Ferry will cut some time off the drive.
  • You can also drive around - stop at Hama Hama Oysters or Union City Market if you go this route. Also don’t watch this Tacoma Narrows Bridge video - that every Washington physics class uses to teach resonance and frequencies - until after you’ve driven over it.
What to do
  • Hoh Rainforest - slaps
  • Olympic National Park - slaps
  • Port Townsend - slaps (skip Port Angeles it kinda sucks)
  • Lavender Farms (if you’re into that)
  • Olympic Game Farm - feed bison bread through your car window, expect enough saliva to need a car wash and a shower after. Slaps?

North Cascades National Park

recommend 2+ nightslate July ~86°/50°F, hot and dry

This is one of our favorite places. Here are just a few hikes we like. You could day trip it, but it’s a 3 hour drive each way so we recommend overnighting somewhere.

What to do
  • Here are some of our favorite hikes
    • Cutthroat Pass
    • Maple Pass
    • Blue Lake
    • Colchuck Lake
    • Ingalls Lake
  • Diablo Lake/Ross Lake - primarily for kayaking although there are some hiking trails from the lake. Can only access Ross Lake via a 1 mile hike. Kayak rentals available on Ross Lake
Where to stay
  • Mazama is our favorite place to go and is where we got engaged. While it is home to some of the best XC skiing in the country, it can get pretty hot in the summer.
    • Accessible via North Cascades highway (very pretty drive and the shorter route of the two ways to get there) in the summer. I-5 north to Burlington, then Highway 20 east over Rainy and Washington Passes. About 180 miles / 4 hours. Directions.
    • If you want to stop in Leavenworth you can take Highway 2 and either Snoqualmie + Blewett Pass or Stevens Pass. If you take this route recommend a half way stop at Anjou (great french bakery). About 250 miles / 5 hours either way. Directions.
    • Mazama Store - a great bakery, grocery store, cafe in town. Highly recommend the baguette.
    • The Inn at Mazama/Cabins of the Methow - has a hotel but also rents many cabins. We have never stayed at the hotel but have rented through them many times for cabins
  • Leavenworth is kind of touristy, but it is a cute and walkable Bavarian town
    • You can float the Wenatchee River right through town! Leavenworth Outdoor Center rents tubes and shuttles you to the put-in (more info).
  • Winthrop while we’re on the subject of themed towns, this one is straight out of an Old Western. Pearrygin Lake State Park has good camping, and there is a lot of gravel biking in the area.

Victoria, BC

Recommend 2 nightslate July ~73°/53°F, warm and dry

Wow, we really do a lot of themed towns in the PNW. Victoria is uhhh… Victorian? I guess? Idk it looks like Charles Dickens Britain. It’s got a really walkable downtown.

How to get there
  • You can take the Victoria Clipper directly from downtown Seattle or check out the Tofino section if you want to bring a car
What to do
  • Explore Butchart Gardens, get high tea at the Fairmont Empress, explore a cute island town with good coffee and breakfast. If you want to drive, see the Tofino section for details.

Tofino, BC

Recommend 4+ nightslate July ~66°/51°F, cool and often foggy

Tofino is a small surfing town known for its storms. There are tons of beaches and beachfront resorts, as well as good restaurants, and of course, coffee.

How to get there
  • There are 2 ways to get to Vancouver Island from Seattle and both will require a car.
    • Drive to Port Angeles, take the Black Ball Ferry to Victoria and drive to Tofino.
    • There is also a ferry from Vancouver BC to Victoria, but be generous with your drive time estimate because the border crossing can be slow (US-bound wait times). Let us know if you want any more information here, we loved Tofino!
What to do
  • Go to Ucluelet for some great waterfront hiking, check out their brewery for great water views and cozy vibes, and book a rez for Pluvio if you like fine dining.
  • Surf at Cox Bay (don’t worry the board shops rent out wetsuits too!)
  • Book a whale watching tour (they leave from downtown)
  • Get fish tacos at Tacofino
  • Stop at Goats on the Roof Market on your way there
  • Hike in Pacific Rim National Park
