Emily's Colors

Auburn-brown hair that goes copper in sun, neutral skin that tans golden, grey-green eyes with a dark rim, a natural rosy lip, and a solid jump in lightness between hair and skin. Rich, warm and earthy beats pale, icy or dusty. The warmth lives in the hair; the skin itself is neutral.

The three dials

Every season is a position on three dials. Hers are read off the anchors above.

Value · how dark
Medium-dark, medium-high contrast
lightdark
Skin lightness 75 to 80 against dry hair at 29 to 31 and eyes at 28 to 42: a 40-point jump. Deep colors hold up well; the very darkest are an option, not a requirement.
Hue · cool to warm
Warm hair, neutral skin
coolwarm
Hair sits at hue 57° with real chroma (auburn). Skin has yellow and red within three points of each other (neutral, not peach-golden), and the eyes are grey. Warm colors flatter because of the hair.
Chroma · muted to clear
Medium, earthy not bright
mutedclear
Eyes carry almost no chroma, hair a medium amount. Rich and earthy works; neon, icy and greyed-out do not.

Wear these

Teals and greens sit opposite the flush and echo the grey-green in her eyes. Rust, pumpkin, terracotta and the wine tones are the hair's auburn turned up, and they are the color-wheel complement of green, so they make the eyes read greener. Everything is deep enough to stand next to the hair, and nothing here is the loud end of Autumn: her grey eyes want earthy versions of these colors, not the bright ones. Tap a swatch to copy its hex.

Pairings that do the work

Her contrast is high, so outfits should carry one deep color and one lighter or brighter one. Two mid-tones together look flat on her.

Metals, hair, lips

WhereYesNo
MetalYellow gold (what she wears), bronze, brass, copper, antiqued gold. Rose gold and tortoiseshell are fine. Bright silver and platinum read cold next to the chestnut; mix them with gold rather than wearing them alone.
HairKeep the natural chestnut. If coloring: warm chocolate or auburn gloss, copper lowlights. Ash, blue-black, and light blonde balayage. Blonde collapses the hair-to-skin contrast the whole palette relies on.
LipsTerracotta, brick, warm berry, and a rose one step warmer than her natural lip. Cool fuchsia, bubblegum pink, anything with a blue cast. (If fuchsia secretly looks great, see the drape test.)
EyesBronze, copper, olive, warm taupe; brown liner reads softer than black and still holds against dark lashes. Silver or icy-lilac shimmer.

Skip these

Pale and icy colors are too light to hold up next to dark hair and drain the skin; dusty or greyed tones cancel her contrast; cool-grey and blue-white fight the warm hair. Neon overwhelms low-chroma eyes.
