Wear these

Teals and greens sit opposite the flush and echo the grey-green in her eyes. Rust, terracotta and the deep wine tones are the hair's sunlit copper turned up, and they are the color-wheel complement of green, so they make the eyes read greener. Everything is deep enough to stand next to dark hair. Tap a swatch to copy its hex.

Pairings that do the work

Her contrast is high, so outfits should carry one deep color and one lighter or brighter one. Two mid-tones together look flat on her.

Metals, hair, lips

WhereYesNot as the only one
MetalYellow gold (what she wears), bronze, brass, antiqued gold. Rose gold is fine. Bright silver and platinum read cold next to the chestnut; mix them with gold rather than wearing them alone.
HairKeep the natural chestnut. If coloring: warm chocolate or auburn gloss, copper lowlights. Ash, blue-black, and light blonde balayage. Blonde collapses the hair-to-skin contrast the whole palette relies on.
LipsTerracotta, brick, warm berry, and a rose one step warmer than her natural lip. Cool fuchsia, bubblegum pink, anything with a blue cast. (If fuchsia secretly looks great, see the drape test.)
EyesBronze, copper, olive, warm taupe; brown liner reads softer than black and still holds against dark lashes. Silver or icy-lilac shimmer.

Skip these

Pale and icy colors are too light to hold up next to dark hair and drain the skin; dusty or greyed tones cancel her contrast; cool-grey and blue-white fight the warm hair. Neon overwhelms low-chroma eyes.

The one test that settles Autumn vs Winter

Photos cannot resolve this, only fabric at the face in daylight can. Hold these in pairs and see which one makes the skin look even and the eyes lighter: tomato red vs fuchsia, dark chocolate vs pure black, cream vs stark white. If the first of each pair wins, this page stands. If fuchsia, black and white win, she is a Dark Winter: keep the depth, drop the rust and marigold, and swap in true red, emerald, royal purple and black.
