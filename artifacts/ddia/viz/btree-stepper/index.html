    How costs are counted. A disk read is one B-tree page or one LSM file. The B-tree root is treated as cached in RAM, the same free ride the memtable and the Bloom filters get. Writing a B-tree page rewrites the whole page, so it costs a full page of entries; a flush or compaction costs the entries it writes. Neither side's write-ahead log is counted, and compaction's own reads are left out.

    What is simplified. The B-tree is the classic Bayer and McCreight design (1972) with values in every page; most databases use the B+ variant, which splits and merges the same way. The LSM-tree has two levels, merges all of L0 into L1 once L0 reaches three files, and its Bloom filters never give false positives (real ones do, about 1% of the time). Both are tiny: a real B-tree page holds hundreds of keys, so real trees are three or four levels deep with everything but the leaves cached, and a real LSM-tree has five to seven levels.
